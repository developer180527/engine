render: the world split — culling, sorting, lights leave the pipeline

`IRenderPipeline` was advertised as THE customization point: swap it, your
game looks different. It was not usable as one. Culling, sort order, batching
and light packing all lived inside `ForwardPipeline`, so a studio wanting a
cartoon look — different shading, nothing else — had to reimplement frustum
culling to get it. That is audit finding R2, rated Critical.

All four move to `src/render/world/` (`rworld::`), one concern per file:

  render_world.h        the frozen frame description, no behaviour
  frustum.h/.cpp        is this volume outside the view?
  sort_key.h            "how should this draw be ordered?" as one uint64
  visibility.h/.cpp     cull -> key -> sort, plus the batch-run predicate
  light_packing.h/.cpp  LightItem[] -> the layout the shader expects

Everything here is GPU-FREE. That is a hard constraint, not tidiness. It is
what makes `tests/render_world_test.cpp` possible — 42 assertions, and the
first test in src/render's history that can fail on a culling or sort-order
regression. Both are things that fail SILENTLY: geometry pops out of existence
at the screen edge, batching quietly collapses to one draw per object, and
neither crashes. Mutation-proved: inverting the opaque key layout and culling
unbounded items each produce failures (9 total); reverting restores PASS.

Two design calls worth recording:

RenderItem carries its own bounds, copied at extraction. Culling used to reach
through `mesh->boundsCenter()` per item per view, and shadow cascades multiply
the view count. Six floats make the cull loop a linear scan over contiguous
PODs — and remove the Mesh dependency that would otherwise drag bgfx in here.

Opaque sorts by MATERIAL before depth. Blend class always leads (opaque before
transparent is correctness). Below that, opaque keeps material above depth
because the pipeline this replaces sorted by (meshKey, matKey) — pure batching
— and flipping to depth-first would trade that away for early-Z on a guess.
Depth rides along as the tiebreaker. Transparent inverts the layout entirely:
depth dominates, far first, or blending is wrong.

Also fixed while moving it: the shadow-light index the shader reads is an index
into the PACKED array, but the pipeline was assigning it from the SOURCE light
list. The two diverge once lights are dropped at the 16-light cap, so a caster
past the cap would have pointed the shader at some other light's direction.

forward_pipeline.h 463 -> 423 lines, and what remains is shaders, uniforms and
binds — which is what a pipeline should be. Three stale TODOs asking for
exactly this work are now deleted rather than described.

Verified: full build clean, ctest unit 26/26 (was 25), engine_doctor 53 docs /
0 errors, fps_shooter runs live — 5 textures resident, 2048 shadow map, physics
and gameplay raycasts intact.
