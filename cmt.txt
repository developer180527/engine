render+cook: measure the SHIPPED path; fix two bugs that hid it

Measured engine_player (cooked/BC) against engine_host (source importer/raw
RGBA8), 400 fixed frames each:
                    textures   render targets   peak
  DEV  engine_host    76.0 MB      71.0 MB     147.1 MB
  SHIP engine_player   0.0 MB      71.0 MB      71.1 MB

The shipped game already fits the 128 MB target machine. Its texture cost is
effectively nil — BC + mips through AssetService — and its whole footprint is
render targets, which measured 71 MB even in a run where NO meshes loaded.
That is fixed overhead, ~57 MB above the naive 1280x720 figure, and is now the
#1 renderer item (audit R6). Draw calls 12.9, zero handle churn.

engine_player gains --frames N and --gpu-stats. A shipped player needs them
because the numbers that matter are measured ON the target machine; they are
opt-in and leave the default shipping posture unchanged.

Two bugs, both introduced by me, both silent:

1. Scenes could no longer be cooked by the CLI AT ALL. fileSettled()'s
   stability map is per-process, so a fresh engine_cook always defers a scene
   on its only pass; scene deferrals were not counted in `deferred`, so
   cookOnce() concluded "up to date" and exited printing "0 cooked". Fixed by
   reporting scene deferrals and assigning m_stats.deferred AFTER the scene
   scan — the previous assignment ran before it and only knew about assets.

2. SceneCooker trusted a stale "cookedPath" cached in the scene JSON,
   consulting the asset DB only when that field was empty. A UUID from a dead
   registry generation was copied through verbatim, producing a scene that
   cooked successfully and loaded NOTHING (every mesh "Cannot stat"). This hits
   any teammate who clones, anyone whose registry.db regenerates, and every
   fresh CI machine. The DB now wins; stale refs are healed and logged.
   Verified: 3/3 refs healed, 0 load failures.
