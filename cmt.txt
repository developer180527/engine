render: shadow resolution is a project quality setting (71 MB -> 23 MB)

SHADOW_SIZE was hardcoded at 4096. That one constant allocated a 4096x4096
D32F depth texture = 64 MB, which measured as 90% of a shipped game's entire
71 MB GPU footprint — more than every mesh and texture in the scene combined,
and instantly fatal on an integrated GPU with ~128 MB.

Now project.json data, alongside the providers block:
    "graphics": { "shadowResolution": 2048 }

Default 2048 (16 MB): sharp enough for a normal scene and 4x cheaper than what
shipped. Measured on fps_shooter, shipped path:
    1024 ->  4 MB shadow -> 11.1 MB total VRAM
    2048 -> 16 MB shadow -> 23.1 MB total   (new default, was 71.1)
    4096 -> 64 MB shadow -> 71.1 MB total   (previous behaviour)

Validated at the project layer, because the cost is quadratic and the failure
is not a crash but a machine quietly running out of VRAM: clamped to
[256, 8192] and rounded DOWN to a power of two (2000 -> 1024; GPUs want POT
depth targets and a typo must not become a driver rejection). Verified all
four paths, including 99999 -> 8192.

Renderer::setShadowResolution works before or after init: a change once the
pipeline is attached re-attaches it, since the shadow map is created at attach
time and never resized. That is project-open, not per-frame.

The size is printed at creation ("Shadow map 2048x2048 D32F = 16.0 MB") — the
engine's largest single allocation should never be a silent decision.
