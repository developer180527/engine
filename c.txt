fuzz: the 20k-50k cliff does not exist, retracting it, with the numbers

Bisected with a bounded driver (per-run timeout recorded as data, so a pathological
size cannot eat the sweep), separating boot+load from per-frame cost by timing
--frames 1 against --frames 11:

  objects   load     ms/entity  frame
   10 000   2.4 s    0.24      
   20 000   4.8 s    0.24       (noise)
   25 000   7.0 s    0.28      
   40 000   14.8 s   0.37       67 ms
   50 000   22.0 s   0.44       93 ms

BOTH CURVES ARE SMOOTH. There is no discontinuity. 25 000 objects, the exact size
that previously timed out past 150 s, load in 7 s, and 50 000 run at 93 ms per frame,
not the >18 s per frame the previous commit message claimed.

RETRACTION. That figure came from runs killed by a tool timeout; contention from those
killed processes is the likely cause, but a check for orphaned processes afterwards
found none, so it cannot be confirmed. The honest statement is that the measurement was
unreliable and the conclusion drawn from it was wrong. Recorded as such in
src/runtime/docs/issues.md rather than dropped, because the wrong number was quoted in
a commit.

A CONFOUND THAT NEARLY BECAME THE ANSWER. The generator was edited mid-sweep to fix
"parent" -> "parentId" (SceneSerializer::restoreParents reads parentId, and an unknown
key is silently ignored, so 15% of every scene generated before that were "children"
that loaded as roots, and the hierarchy layer was inert with nothing saying so). That
made 25 000 the first size with real parenting, which alone could explain a jump, and
ChildOf being an archetype pair made "table explosion" a plausible story. Measured
instead of believed: 25 000 objects load in 6.94 s with no children and 7.04 s with
15% children. Hierarchy is not a factor.

THE REAL FINDING: scene LOAD dominates and is mildly superlinear (~N^1.35). Per-entity
cost nearly doubles from 10 k to 50 k, so opening a 50 000-object scene takes 22
seconds, while the whole renderer, extraction through submit, is single-digit
milliseconds at the same sizes. 0.24 ms to create ONE entity with four components is
already two orders of magnitude off what flecs can do, and the superlinearity sits on
top of that.

Deliberately NOT diagnosed further. Candidates, none confirmed: nlohmann's DOM for a
27 MB scene (materialised whole before the first entity is created, string-compare key
lookups), per-entity archetype moves from setting components one at a time, and
per-entity asset resolution. A sampling profiler on a --frames 1 run of the 50 k scene
settles it in one shot; that is the next step rather than more speculation.
