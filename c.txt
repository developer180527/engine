render: the cull reads 24-byte SoA streams, not the 144-byte RenderItem

RenderItem is 144 bytes and the cull touched offsets 0..141, all three of its cache
lines, to recover a bounding sphere and two ids. CullStreams (world/render_world.h)
is the SoA alternative: parallel x/y/z/r/keyBase arrays, 24 bytes per item, filled by
extraction while the model matrix is still in registers.

THE BIGGER REASON WAS NOT THE BYTE COUNT. The world bounding sphere DOES NOT DEPEND ON
THE CAMERA, yet it was rebuilt per view, once against the camera frustum and again
against the light's. The shadow pass was recomputing every sphere the camera pass had
just computed. Building it once at extraction is what that pass's 0.25 -> 0.09 ms is.

MEASURED, three-run medians:

                       before    after
  50 k Render.shadow   0.25 ms   0.09
  50 k Render.cull     0.94      0.83
  50 k Render.extract  2.69      2.89
  50 k Render          4.14      4.06
  100 k Render.shadow  0.48      0.15
  100 k Render.cull    1.62      1.34
  100 k Render.extract 5.37      5.54
  100 k Render         7.80      7.42

NET ~2% AT 50 K, ~5% AT 100 K, smaller than the byte arithmetic implies. Extraction 
absorbed most of what the cull and shadow passes gave up,
because the work MOVED rather than disappeared: extraction now computes the sphere and
writes five streams. Only the duplication genuinely vanished. Both phases were already
parallel across 12 cores, so the bandwidth being saved was already spread, the same
conclusion the earlier 4% sqrt result pointed at.

What it DID buy, and the reason to keep it: the cull now reads four contiguous float
arrays, which is the precondition for a 4-wide test. Writing NEON first against the
144-byte AoS layout would have meant deinterleaving four items out of three cache lines
each before any arithmetic, the LAYOUT, not the instruction set, was in the way.

TWO ENCODINGS to know before touching it. The radius carries both sentinels so the hot
loop needs no side table: r < 0 is 'not renderable', r == infinity is 'renderable but
unbounded, never culled' (an unbounded mesh is a missing-data problem; dropping it
silently would read as a renderer bug). And the sort key is SPLIT, extraction packs
material+mesh (opaqueKeyBase), the cull ORs in depth (withOpaqueDepth), with
render_world_test asserting the split form equals the single-shot makeSortKey over
4 000 randomised triples plus the extremes. A one-bit disagreement would reorder draws
and break batching while producing perfectly plausible keys.

buildVisibleSet REQUIRES the streams; there is deliberately no fallback that rebuilds
them from the items, because a fallback is the path the tests exercise while the fused
one ships. Callers that are not extraction use rworld::fillCullStream, and both go
through the same writeCullEntry, one definition of the rule, not two.

Extraction's compaction now moves items AND streams together. They are indexed
alongside each other, so a partial move would have the cull reading one object's bounds
for another's, silently.
