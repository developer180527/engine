runtime: bone palettes out of the ECS component, 125 MB to 0.2 MB

SkinnedMesh held `float skinMatrices[128*16]` inline, making the component
8 200 bytes: 64 cache lines, larger than a page. The palette now lives in
anim::skinPalettes() and the component keeps a 4-byte slot, 8 200 B -> 12 B.

At horde scale that is 125.1 MB -> 0.2 MB for 16 000 skinned entities, which
is the whole justification. On a stated 4 GB target, 8 KB per zombie is not a
rounding error.

── the speed claim I made was WRONG, and the harness caught it ─────────────
I predicted a large extraction win from a microbenchmark that measured a
SINGLE-component query, where the 8 KB stride was the only cost. The real
extraction query is <Transform, MeshRenderer, SkinnedMesh>, flecs columns each
component separately, and the per-entity callback overhead (~19 ns) swamps the
stride entirely. Measured on the real component and the real query shape:

    N=4 000    read 18.9 -> 18.8 ns/entity
    N=16 000   read 21.0 -> 19.2 ns/entity
    write      ~1030 ns/entity, unchanged (same bytes either way)

Flat whether the component is 8 200 bytes or 12. The 15.8x does not transfer,
and the alignment effect I attributed to the write path did not reproduce
either. Recorded here so nobody re-derives that number from the microbenchmark
later and wonders why the frame never got faster.

This is the entire reason for measuring on a real scene rather than a
microbenchmark, and the harness falsified the headline claim on its first run.

── stress_skinned, a gap that existed independently ────────────────────────
The skinned path had NO load coverage: stress_swarm, stress_churn and
stress_physics all use static meshes, so the one component measured in
kilobytes per entity was never exercised at scale. It drives both real access
patterns, extraction's five-byte read and the animator's full-palette write,
hermetically, no assets, no ozz, no GPU.

── pool details that are not incidental ────────────────────────────────────
Storage is CHUNKED and never reallocated: extraction hands the raw palette
pointer to the render item and the GPU upload reads it later in the frame, so a
growing std::vector would invalidate live pointers at the worst moment. Slots
recycle through a free list, are acquired lazily on first animation, and are
released by a SkinnedMesh on_remove hook, without which a spawn-and-kill horde
leaks 8 KB a time. A world that never registers the hook leaks slots: bounded,
never unsafe, memory rather than correctness, and said so in the header.

animator_system_test now resolves palettes the way the renderer does, so it
still asserts on real contents rather than on a field that moved.

64 tests pass. fps_shooter renders identically.
