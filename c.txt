render: RenderItem 144 -> 128 bytes, and two of them were write-only

Found looking for the next lever after the cull stopped reading RenderItem at all.

const Material* mat and const Texture* tex WERE WRITE-ONLY. Extraction resolved both
handles and stored the pointers; nothing ever read them, because
ForwardPipeline::bindMaterial re-resolves through ctx.materials/ctx.textures. They
cost 16 bytes per item plus two registry lookups per item per frame for nothing, and
they were a SECOND SOURCE OF TRUTH for an item's material, which is a stale-cache bug
waiting for the first code that mutates a material mid-frame.

Those are exactly the lookups an earlier differential measured at 0.17 ms per 20 000
items and filed under 'measured NOT to matter, so nobody spends a day on them'. The
conclusion was right and the reason was wrong: not a small cost worth keeping, a cost
buying nothing.

Removed, and the remainder repacked, matrix and pointers first, the extraction-only
block (bounds, keys, material handle) after, small scalars grouped instead of each
padding out to 8. 144 -> 128 bytes: exactly two cache lines, for a struct the submit
path reads by RANDOM index since draws are visited in sorted order.

  Render.extract, 3-run medians    144 B     128 B
  50 000 objects                   2.866 ms  2.859  (flat)
  100 000 objects                  5.518     5.342  (-3.2%)

WHY 3% AND NOT 15%: the part worth keeping. The old differential predicted ~0.85 ms
saved at 100 000; the actual saving is 0.18. That is not measurement error: THAT
DIFFERENTIAL RAN AGAINST SERIAL EXTRACTION. Extraction is now jobs::parallelFor across
12 cores, so a per-item cost divides by the core count before it reaches wall time,
0.17 ms of serial work is ~0.014 ms of frame time.

Which reframes the remaining backlog, and explains why the last several changes each
returned 2-5% where the same edits would have returned far more three commits ago:
once a phase is parallel, per-item savings are worth roughly 1/cores of what they look
like, and the cheap wins are spent. The next real lever is NOT EXTRACTING AT ALL for
entities that did not change, incremental extraction off flecs change detection,
which is a design change and should begin by measuring how static a typical frame's
item set is rather than assuming most of it is.

Bounds and keys stay on RenderItem deliberately: rworld::writeCullEntry takes a
RenderItem, so the item is the input the streams are derived from. Splitting
submit-only fields from stream-source fields would shrink it further at the cost of
two structures to keep parallel, not worth it while submission never touches the cold
block.

51/51 ctest, submit counters byte-identical at 50 k (31128 culled / 18873 instanced).

extract.cpp's header where the next person will look.
