render: submesh-granular visible sets, 3067 draws + 534 refused becomes 299

R17 named this as the prerequisite for both instancing and material-bind dedup on real
content. VisibleDraw now carries a submesh ordinal, ranges are expanded into their own
draws BEFORE the sort, and each range's key holds ITS OWN material.

50 000 real cooked props: the case that previously could not be drawn at all:

                    before                     after
  draws             3 067 + 534 REFUSED        299
  frame             INCOMPLETE (ceiling hit)   complete, ceiling not reached
  instanced         0 submits                  299 covering 41 571 items
  material binds    3 067                      299

5 000 props: 2 869 -> 299 draws and binds; instanced 58 submits covering 966 items ->
299 covering 4 884. Synthetic cubes unchanged at 1 draw / 1 bind, the control that
shows expansion costs nothing when there is nothing to expand.

WHERE THE EXPANSION LIVES. In the pipeline, not rworld, because that is where Mesh* is
legal: world/ never dereferences a Mesh, and that is the whole reason culling and
sorting are unit-testable without bgfx. Cost is one pass plus one extra radix sort over
~1.7x the entries (Render.expand 3.8 ms at 50 k, against ~41 000 submits removed);
content with no submeshes skips both.

batchRunLength HAD TO LEARN ABOUT SUBMESHES, and this is not optional: two ranges of one
mesh that share a material produce EQUAL keys, material and mesh are all the key holds,
while needing different index ranges. Instancing them together would draw one range's
indices for the other's geometry.

SKINNED ITEMS ARE NOT EXPANDED, AND THE TEST CAUGHT IT. The first version expanded
everything and render_pipeline_test failed immediately: one skinned item with 4 ranges
produced 4 bone-palette uploads instead of 1, re-introducing exactly the bug R4 fixed.
Structural, not a slip, a palette is a per-ITEM uniform relied upon to persist across
that item's submits, and expansion scatters an item's ranges so other items' palettes
land in between; deduping would render with the wrong bones. Skinned draws are excluded
from instancing anyway, so expansion buys them nothing and they keep whole-item entries
plus their submesh loop at submit. R4's invariant holds unchanged.

THE BOTTLENECK MOVED. At 50 000 real props the frame is 43.9 ms with Render 27.3, of
which EXTRACTION is 20.5, against 2.83 for 50 000 cubes. Real content is ~7x more
expensive per item: 176 distinct Mesh dereferences instead of one shared primitive,
PrevTransform interpolation actually running (25% of the scene moves), and 15% of items
taking the parented ancestor walk. Submission is no longer the wall for either content
shape; extraction is.

STILL OPEN: the shadow pass keeps the old submeshes.empty() exclusion and its inner loop
— 322 draws with 28 instanced submits at 50 k. Out of scope to keep one change
reviewable. It can go further than the colour pass ever can: depth-only means material
identity is irrelevant, so a mesh's ranges could collapse into ONE draw over the whole
index buffer, if submeshes partition it contiguously. Check that first.

Also adds docs/architecture/renderer-vs-production.md, written this session to answer
'is bgfx the bottleneck, did we make an architectural mistake' with numbers: no on both
counts, bgfx's own draw limit is 65 535, our 4 096 guards Metal's unchecked 8 MB uniform
scratch, and bgfx already supports compute, indirect draws and multi-threaded encoders
that we do not use. The doc's own work list is updated in the same commit so it does not
ship stale: item 1 is this change, and the remaining order is LOD, then indirect +
bindless, then multi-threaded encoders.
