render: renderer.cpp was four jobs in one file, now four files

437 lines holding bgfx device init, framebuffer lifetime, the flecs extraction
loop, and pipeline attach/detach. Split along the same lines as pipeline/, since
these are all Renderer methods and belong under a directory named for the class:

  renderer.cpp           83  pipeline ownership: attach/detach, makeContext
  renderer/device.cpp   171  bgfx up/down + the Rendering-heap allocator
  renderer/targets.cpp  125  framebuffers + the three render* entry points
  renderer/extract.cpp  124  ECS -> RenderView, alone

The grouping is not arbitrary. What is LEFT in renderer.cpp is one recurring
hazard and nothing else: the pipeline builds its programs and targets in
onAttach, once, so any setting they depend on must re-attach when it changes
after init. That is the whole reason setShadowResolution and setShaderCacheRoot
are more than field writes, and forgetting it does not fail loudly, it silently
keeps the old programs, which is how the cooked-shader path once no-oped
entirely. Both setters now sit together with that stated once at the top.

extract.cpp is the one that matters next. ~38 ms per frame at 20 000 objects WITH
A SINGLE DRAW CALL, all of it per item: query iteration, world-matrix lerp, three
registry lookups, try_get<SkinnedMesh>, push_back. The file says so, so the next
person profiling does not have to rediscover that draw submission stopped being
the bottleneck and this replaced it.

TWO CHANGES BEYOND MOVING TEXT, both so targets.cpp is about targets rather than
framebuffer bookkeeping:

  destroyTargets(), the six-handle teardown createSceneFB and shutdown had each

  ensureGameFB(), 20 lines of lazy creation lifted out of renderGameView.
